## What the DASH diet is

DASH stands for Dietary Approaches to Stop Hypertension. It was tested in controlled trials in which participants were handed their meals.[^appel1997] The pattern shifts the whole plate rather than removing one nutrient: more potassium, magnesium, calcium and fibre, less sodium and saturated fat.

## What the research shows

In the DASH-Sodium trial, combining the DASH pattern with the lowest salt level lowered systolic pressure by about 11.5 mmHg in people with hypertension and about 7 mmHg in people with normal pressure.[^sacks2001] That is about the size of one blood-pressure tablet.

Real life is less tidy than a feeding trial. When the results of many trials are pooled, DASH on its own lowers systolic pressure by roughly 3 to 5 mmHg.[^filippou2020][^siervo2015] That is still useful, and it is a low-risk change that adds to whatever else you are doing.

:::figure fig-bp.svg | The largest effects come from a feeding trial in which meals were provided and salt was cut as well. Pooled results from everyday trials are smaller but consistent. Bars show the fall in systolic pressure compared with a typical diet.

Large observational studies also link closer adherence to fewer cardiovascular events and lower mortality.[^chiavaroli2019][^soltani2020] Those studies show an association, not proof that the diet caused it.

## How to eat it

Daily targets, in servings (a serving is about a fist of vegetables, a piece of fruit, or a slice of bread):

| Food group | Servings | What counts |
|---|---|---|
| Vegetables | 4–5 a day | Fresh, frozen, any colour |
| Fruit | 4–5 a day | Whole fruit more often than juice |
| Whole grains | 6–8 a day | Oats, brown rice, wholemeal bread, whole-wheat pasta |
| Low-fat dairy | 2–3 a day | Milk, yoghurt, kefir, low-salt cottage cheese |
| Nuts, seeds, legumes | Several times a week | Lentils, beans, chickpeas, almonds, sunflower seeds (skip nuts if you are allergic) |
| Red and processed meat, sweets, sugary drinks | Keep small | Sausage, bacon, soda, sweets |

### Salt is the key lever

Aim for under 2,300 mg of sodium a day (about one level teaspoon of salt, counting everything), and move towards 1,500 mg if you can.[^sacks2001] Most sodium does not come from the salt shaker. It comes from bread, cheese, sausage, canned and packaged foods, sauces and restaurant meals. Cooking from whole ingredients and tasting before salting does most of the work.

:::plan Example day
**Breakfast.** Porridge made with milk, a sliced banana, a spoonful of walnuts.

**Snack.** An apple and a small pot of plain yoghurt.

**Lunch.** Lentil and vegetable soup made without stock cubes, a slice of wholemeal bread, a large salad dressed with olive oil and lemon.

**Snack.** Carrot sticks and a handful of unsalted almonds.

**Dinner.** Baked chicken or fish with herbs, brown rice, steamed broccoli and tomato.

**Flavour without salt:** lemon, garlic, onion, pepper, paprika, dill, parsley, basil, vinegar.
:::

:::callout tip Easy swaps
- Plain yoghurt with fruit instead of flavoured yoghurt.
- Home-cooked chicken instead of sliced deli meat.
- Unsalted nuts instead of crisps.
- A tin of beans, rinsed under the tap, instead of ready meals. Cheese and cured foods are salty: take small portions.
- Herbs and lemon instead of the second pinch of salt.
:::

## Who should be careful

:::callout safety Check with your doctor first if any of this applies
- **Kidney disease, or a high potassium level.** The standard DASH diet is deliberately rich in potassium, magnesium and phosphorus. People with advanced kidney disease need individual limits.
- **Potassium-sparing diuretics, ACE inhibitors, ARBs or aldosterone blockers.** These raise potassium. Do not add salt substitutes (they are made of potassium chloride) without asking.
- **You take blood-pressure medicine.** Pressure can fall further as the diet works. Check it at home and tell your doctor if it is low or you feel dizzy. Do not stop medicine on your own.
- **You take insulin or a sulfonylurea.** A big change in what you eat can cause low blood sugar; tell your doctor before you start.
- **You take lithium or warfarin.** A sudden cut in salt can raise lithium levels, and a sudden increase in leafy greens changes how warfarin works. Tell your doctor before making a large change.
- **You are allergic to nuts.** Leave them out of the example day.
- **A sensitive gut.** The extra fibre can cause bloating for the first weeks; increase gradually.
:::

## How to tell it is working

Measure your blood pressure at home, at the same time of day, a few mornings a week. Expect movement within 2 to 4 weeks. Your doctor may also look at cholesterol, HbA1c and, if you take the medicines above, potassium and kidney function.

## Common mistakes

- Cutting salt at the table but not noticing it in bread, cheese and sausage.
- Treating DASH as a diet to finish. The effect lasts only while the pattern does.
- Replacing meat with sweets or refined carbohydrates. The pattern works because of what you add, not only what you remove.

## How strong is the evidence

**Grade A.** The evidence is unusually strong: controlled feeding trials, pooled analyses of many trials, an umbrella review and a place in the US hypertension guideline.[^jones2025] The main caveat is that most trials were short (weeks to a few months), so the long-term benefit for heart attacks and strokes rests on observational data. Real-world effects are smaller than the best trial result, because in everyday life it is harder to follow the pattern exactly.

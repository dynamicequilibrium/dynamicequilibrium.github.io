Who this is for

Capable adults who want to understand what is going on and what to do about it — whether they arrive with a diagnosis, a folder of test results, or only a sense that something is off.

Tiredness that does not lift

Low energy, poor sleep, waking unrefreshed — when the usual explanations have not settled it.

Stress, anxiety and low mood

Tension that does not switch off, a heavy or flat mood, burnout, the feeling of running on autopilot.

Midlife and hormonal change

Perimenopause, shifts in energy and mood — and the questions worth bringing to your own doctor.

A diagnosis, or results nobody has put together

Living with a diagnosis, or holding lab results, reports and advice from several doctors that you would like read together as one story.

Performing, at a cost

Still delivering, but on willpower: broken sleep, wired-and-tired evenings, caffeine or alcohol to take the edge off, decisions that cost more than they used to.

Relationships and meaning

Strain at home, loss, a transition, or a quiet question about what all the effort is for.

Many people I see carry a great deal — founders, executives, physicians, parents, athletes — and are already under a doctor’s care. This work sits alongside that care and does not replace it. The underlying pattern is usually the same: a capable system that has been running past its limits.

Health does not come in compartments

Symptoms rarely stay in one place. Poor sleep feeds anxiety, strain in a relationship shows up in the body, a long stretch without meaning drains energy that no supplement restores. I look at five threads together.

Body

History, sleep, nutrition, hormones, digestion, test results.

Emotional life

Mood, anxiety, stress, and how you cope with them.

Behaviour

Habits and routines — what is realistic in your life.

Relationships

The people who support or exhaust you, at home and at work.

Meaning

What matters to you, and whether your days reflect it.

The aim is not to address everything at once, but to see which thread, pulled first, loosens the others.

How we work

Four steps, adapted to the person. There is no fixed programme length.

  1. Understand your situation

    Usually before we meet you complete a short written questionnaire and send what you already have: records, results, a list of your medicines and supplements. Then we go through your history, your days and what you have already tried.

  2. Clinical picture and priorities

    I read your history, symptoms, diagnoses and results as a whole: what fits together, what does not, what is likely driving the picture and what is worth clarifying — with your own doctors where needed. Then we decide what matters most, what can wait, and whether further testing would change a decision.

  3. Build an individual plan

    A small number of steps, ordered by likely impact and fitted to your schedule, written down so you can return to it. What belongs with your own doctor, we say so plainly.

  4. Review and adjust

    We look at what worked, what did not and why, and change the plan accordingly.

Questions

Is this a medical service?

It is a consultation with a physician, not a medical service in the sense of regulated care. It runs alongside the care of your own doctors and does not replace formal diagnosis, treatment, prescribing or emergency care. What it does offer is a physician’s reading of your situation: what connects with what, what is likely, what is worth clarifying, and what to take to your treating doctor.

Can I come with a diagnosis?

Yes, and many people do. We look at the diagnosis together with your history and results: what it may mean for your daily life, what could be sustaining it, what is worth clarifying, and what to discuss with the doctor who treats it.

Do I need a diagnosis, or to see a doctor first?

No diagnosis is needed. If a symptom is new, severe or worrying, please see a doctor first, or at the same time. If I think something needs medical attention, I will say so.

What happens in a consultation?

We talk through your situation, your history and what you have already tried, and look at any records or results you have. By the end we aim to have a short list of priorities and agreed next steps, which I put in writing.

What if I take medication or already see specialists?

Keep taking your medication as prescribed. I do not change or stop prescriptions made by other doctors. If a question touches on a medicine, I help you formulate it for the doctor who prescribes it.

Will I need lab tests?

Not necessarily. We start with what you already have. If more testing could change a decision, we discuss it, and it is arranged through you or your own doctor.

Which format should I choose?

A single consultation suits a clear question. If your situation has several layers, monthly support or the ten-session package give time to try a plan and adjust it. Describe your situation in the request and I will suggest one.

How quickly will I see results?

I cannot promise results or a timeline; people differ, and some changes take weeks or months. What I can offer is a clear picture, a short list of priorities, and a way to judge for yourself whether they are working.

How is this different from therapy or coaching?

It is neither. It is not psychotherapy and not life coaching. It is a consultation that starts from the body and your medical history and keeps the rest in view. If you need psychotherapy or psychiatric care, I will tell you so.

Is it confidential? Are sessions recorded?

What you share is treated as confidential and used only for your consultation, as described in the Privacy Policy. A session is recorded or transcribed for my notes only with your agreement, and you may decline.

Which languages and countries?

I work online in English and Russian with people in many countries.

What if it is an emergency?

This is not an emergency service, and I am not available for urgent situations. If you are in danger or may harm yourself, contact local emergency services at once (112 in the EU, 911 in the US) or a crisis line in your country.
